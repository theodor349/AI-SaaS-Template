# Legal documents and acceptance

The privacy, terms, and cookie documents are shared Markdown in [content/legal](../../content/legal), filled in from the app config, `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL`, and two registries: the [consent service registry](../../lib/consent/services.ts) for the cookie table and analytics section and [subprocessors](../../lib/legal/subprocessors.ts) for the privacy policy. The [template syntax](../../lib/legal/template.ts) throws on any unknown name, and the pages render while prerendering, so a typo or a missing `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` fails the build. When a service starts storing anything in the browser, or a new provider processes personal data, register it there; the documents follow.

Creating an account accepts the current terms and privacy notice. The terms say so ("You accept them when you start using"), the sign-up page says so beside Clerk's form, and [`syncProfile`](../../convex/account/users.ts) records the versions and time in `users.legalAcceptance` when it creates the user, so a new buyer goes from sign-up straight to checkout. The sign-up page is the only place that notice appears, so sign-up entry points link to it rather than opening Clerk's modal. After a material change, and for accounts created before this record existed, users accept where it's needed: on `/app`, on `/checkout`, and in a dialog when they choose a plan on the billing page. Each accepted document's front matter `version` must equal [legalVersions](../../convex/account/legalVersions.ts), the versions Convex records and checks; a unit test fails when they differ. Bump both only for a material change, because it asks every user to accept again. Corrections only change `effective`.

Until a user accepts the current versions, [requirePro](../../convex/billing/lib.ts) rejects paid product functions and `polar.checkout` refuses to start a purchase. Account, billing management, export, and deletion must stay available, so don't put those behind `requirePro`. The terms promise this and offer a refund of prepaid time to users who decline.

Section 4 of the terms states that checkout doesn't ask buyers to waive the 14-day withdrawal right, so withdrawal always gives a full refund. If checkout ever adds an express request to start the service immediately, rewrite that section and the [withdrawal flow](../../convex/billing/withdrawals.ts) to match: Danish consumer law then allows a proportionate charge, and the buyer needs a confirmation on a durable medium.

## Withdrawal function

Forbrugeraftaleloven §20 a (in force 19 June 2026) requires a withdrawal function in the online interface, a confirmation step, and an acknowledgement on a durable medium with the withdrawal's content and the time it was received. Polar is the seller, but neither its Buyer Terms nor its checkout and customer portal provide one, so the app does. Whether Polar handles §20 a for merchants is still unanswered; ask Polar and record the answer here. Counsel should review the flow together with the legal text.

- The labels are English, "Withdraw from contract" and "Confirm withdrawal", because the app is English-only. The law asks for "Fortryd aftale" and "Bekræft fortrydelse" or similarly unambiguous wording.
- [The rules](../../convex/billing/withdrawalPeriod.ts) treat a subscription as one purchase from its first paid order. Renewals and plan switches belong to it, so a renewal never opens a new period, and withdrawing refunds every paid order and revokes the subscription. The period ends at Danish midnight after the 14th day following the purchase day. Under §19, stk. 6, a last day that is a Saturday, a public holiday (including Sundays), Constitution Day, Christmas Eve, or New Year's Eve moves to the next working day.
- The receipt goes through the Resend component from the same mutation that records the withdrawal. Hosted deployments must set `RESEND_API_KEY` and `EMAIL_FROM`; without them the withdrawal is still received and refunded, but the receipt isn't sent, and the event asks an operator to send it. Local worktrees never send email.
- A purchase paid entirely with a discount code has nothing to refund. Its withdrawal records `nothingCharged`, and the receipt and completion emails say that access ends without a refund instead of promising one. Counsel should review that wording with the rest.
- Account erasure keeps a withdrawal until its refund is made, because the refund is still owed.
- Every purchase is its own contract with its own 14 days, so a user who buys, withdraws, and buys again can't be refused. Instead, a user's second and later withdrawals email `OPERATOR_ALERT_EMAIL` so the operator can look into it. Detection is per account, so it misses a user who deletes their account and signs up again.
