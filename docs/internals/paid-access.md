# Paid access and provider recovery

Authentication and paid access have separate boundaries. Account provisioning, billing recovery, profile controls, and sign-out remain available without Pro. Paid application functions use [requirePro](../../convex/lib/billing.ts); queries use materialized expiry maintained by durable scheduled mutations, while mutations also check the deadline at execution. New paid features must use this same guard. [PaidBoundary](../../components/billing/paid-boundary.tsx) prevents the UI from mounting paid consumers while readiness or entitlement is absent; it cannot replace backend authorization.

[Polar webhooks](../../convex/http.ts) verify the original body with the pinned SDK and validate the resource association. They commit a reconciliation request through the shared delivery processor. Their payloads never directly grant access. [Reconciliation](../../convex/polar.ts) reads current provider resources, and [billing state](../../convex/billingState.ts) publishes only a current leased snapshot. The entitlement rules in this document live in the pure [computeEntitlement](../../convex/lib/entitlement.ts); billing state only loads and persists around it. Permanent order/subscription IDs and supersession survive delivery retention. Lifetime purchases permanently supersede prior recurring subscriptions, even if the lifetime order is later refunded. An older subscription cannot become an accidental recovery plan.

`billingAccounts` holds only the customer link and the published entitlement, because `billing.status` and `requirePro` subscribe to it from every paid page. Leases, retries, timed checks, delivery tracking, and the operation lock live in `billingSync`, and failures reach the billing page through the separate `billing.syncStatus` query. Write the entitlement row only when the projection changes; each write bumps `revision`. The billing page confirms a subscription change by waiting for a newer revision that matches the request, so `changeSubscription` bumps the revision itself when reconciliation published nothing new, such as cancelling an already cancelled renewal.

A paid recurring order must cover the current subscription period and configured product; order creation, trialing, and unpaid renewals grant no access. Cancellation keeps the paid period. Past-due state ends access without grace; owners recover payment through the portal or checkout. Partial refunds preserve access. A full recurring refund for the current period ends access and durably revokes that subscription; previous-period refunds do not remove a later paid period. A full lifetime refund removes that order's entitlement, preserving any separate unrefunded lifetime purchase.

Customers use a worktree-scoped external identity derived from the authenticated token identifier. Email equality never proves ownership. Customer/order events before signup are retained unclaimed; a later verified external-ID association or owner reconciliation claims them. Missing external IDs must be repaired in Polar with the trustworthy application identity. `billing.recoveryIssues` exposes unclaimed customers, failed reconciliation, and recurring cleanup. Provider failures cannot silently mark cleanup complete. Account deletion consumers call `polar.cleanupAccount` after requesting deletion and retry false results or failures before finishing account cleanup. It checks Polar even when the local customer linkage was missing.

Webhooks, billing actions, account cleanup, and the expiry job at `paidThrough` each request an immediate reconciliation. Each one costs several Polar calls and lists up to 1000 orders, so timed checks are limited to accounts where something can change without a delivered event. Active accounts get a daily safety check for lost refund, revocation, or renewal-flag events. A live subscription without paid time is waiting on a renewal or dunning payment; it is checked hourly during the first day after payment became due, then daily, until Polar ends it. Other ended or unpaid accounts have no timer: a purchase produces a webhook, and checkout reconciles first. A failed reconciliation retries itself with exponential backoff eight times, then the sweep retries it hourly. The per-minute sweep pages through every due account and reschedules itself until it has caught up, so the number of accounts sets its work rather than a fixed per-run limit.

Local bootstrap accepts three distinct sandbox product IDs through `POLAR_PRODUCT_MONTHLY`, `POLAR_PRODUCT_YEARLY`, and `POLAR_PRODUCT_LIFETIME`, validates their fixed positive prices and intervals, and refreshes the public catalog. Hosted deployments need those IDs plus `APP_URL`, `WORKTREE_ID` (a stable deployment namespace), `POLAR_ENVIRONMENT`, `POLAR_ACCESS_TOKEN`, and `POLAR_WEBHOOK_SECRET`. Changing a deployment namespace or product IDs on a live deployment requires reconciling existing associations; don't reuse another deployment's namespace. Foreign worktree resources are ignored before creating domain or retry records. Renewals, refunds, and cleanup remain tied to the scoped customer, rather than trusting order metadata to survive every provider event.
